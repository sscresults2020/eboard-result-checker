SSC Board Challenge 2026: Re-Scrutiny Application

If a subject came back lower than you were sure of, you can ask the board to re-check it. Here is the full re-scrutiny process, the SMS format, the fee and the deadline.

Updated 28 July 2026

Every year, once results are out, some students open their marksheet and find a subject that feels wrong, a grade lower than they were confident about. If that happens to you after the SSC Result 2026, you have a formal option: the board challenge, also called re-scrutiny or khata challenge. This page explains exactly what it does, how to apply by SMS from a Teletalk SIM, what it costs, when the window opens, and how to check the outcome, so you can decide calmly whether it is worth applying.

SECTION 01What the Board Challenge Actually Does

It is important to understand what re-scrutiny is, because many students expect more from it than it offers. The board does not re-mark your paper or grade it again from scratch. Instead, it re-checks the mechanics of your existing script. Specifically, it verifies three things: whether every answer was marked and none were skipped, whether the marks were added correctly, and whether the total was carried over accurately to the result. In practice, the grades that change are almost always the result of an addition error or an answer that was accidentally left unmarked.

This is why most applications do not change a grade. If your paper was marked fully and totalled correctly, re-scrutiny confirms the same result. So the honest advice is to apply where you have a genuine reason to doubt a specific subject, not to challenge every paper in the hope of a lucky bump.

SECTION 02When Can You Apply for Re-Scrutiny in 2026?

The application window opens the day after results are published and usually runs for about a week. With the SSC Result 2026 confirmed for 10 August 2026, you can expect the board challenge window to open around 11 August 2026 and close roughly a week later. In 2025, for comparison, the window ran from 11 to 17 July. The exact dates for 2026 will be set in each board's re-scrutiny notice, so confirm them on your board website before you apply.

The window is short. It is only about a week, and it does not reopen. If a subject looks wrong, decide quickly, keep your marksheet handy, and apply within the dates rather than leaving it to the last day.

SECTION 03How to Apply for the SSC Board Challenge 2026 by SMS

The entire application is done by SMS from a Teletalk prepaid SIM. You do not need to visit the board office. If you do not own a Teletalk SIM, you can apply from a shop that offers the service. Make sure the SIM has enough balance for the fee before you start. The process has two SMS steps.

Step 1: send your application

Open your messaging app and type the message in this format, then send it to 16222:

RSC <BOARD> <ROLL> <SUBJECT CODE>

Here <BOARD> is the first three letters of your board, <ROLL> is your roll number, and <SUBJECT CODE> is the code of the subject you want re-checked. For example, a Dhaka Board student with roll 123456 challenging Mathematics (code 109) would send RSC DHA 123456 109. To challenge more than one subject, separate the codes with a comma, for example RSC DHA 123456 101,109.

Step 2: confirm and pay

You will receive a return SMS with a PIN and the total fee. If you agree, send a second SMS in this format to 16222 to confirm:

RSC YES <PIN> <CONTACT NUMBER>

The contact number can be any operator, not only Teletalk. After you confirm, the fee is deducted and you receive a confirmation SMS with a tracking number. Keep that message, since it is your proof of application.

SECTION 05Fee and Subject Codes

The fee is about 150 Taka per subject, deducted from your Teletalk balance, plus a small SMS charge. An important detail on subjects with two papers: a subject like Bangla or English has a first and second paper, but it counts as one subject for the challenge. You apply using the first paper code, and the fee covers both papers. So keep enough balance for the number of subjects you plan to challenge, and remember that challenging three subjects means roughly 450 Taka.

Each subject has its own code, and you must enter the correct one. You can find the full list of subject codes on your board's website or on your marksheet. Common examples include Bangla first paper as 101 and English first paper as 107. Verify the code before you send, because an incorrect code can waste the application.

Save on charges: if you are challenging several subjects, put all the codes in a single SMS separated by commas rather than sending one message per subject. It is the same fee per subject but fewer SMS charges.

SECTION 06How to Check the Board Challenge Result

The re-scrutiny result is not instant. Boards typically publish it a few weeks after the application window closes, as a PDF on each board's official website. To check, visit your board site, find the SSC board challenge result link, download the PDF and search for your roll number.

There is one detail worth knowing so you are not confused. If your marks did not change, your roll number will not appear in the PDF. The list contains only the students whose results were revised. So not finding your roll usually means your original result stands. If your marks did change, your GPA and marksheet are updated accordingly, and the change is also sent to the contact number you provided during application.

SECTION 07Should You Apply? An Honest View

Re-scrutiny is worth using, but only with realistic expectations. Apply when you have a specific, well founded doubt: a subject you were genuinely confident about that came back surprisingly low, or a result that does not match your performance in the pre-test and model exams. In those cases the small fee is a fair price for peace of mind, and errors do get corrected every year, with some students even moving from fail to pass after re-scrutiny.

On the other hand, challenging a subject simply because you are disappointed, with no particular reason to suspect an error, usually ends with the same grade and a lighter wallet. Read your full marksheet first, pick only the subjects that truly surprise you, and apply for those. SECTION 08Frequently Asked Questions

What is the SSC board challenge?
The board challenge, also called re-scrutiny or khata challenge, is a formal request to have your answer script re-checked. The board does not re-mark the paper. It verifies that every answer was marked, the marks were added correctly, and the total was carried over accurately.
How do I apply for the SSC board challenge 2026?
Apply by SMS from a Teletalk prepaid SIM. Type RSC, then the first three letters of your board, your roll number and the subject code, and send to 16222. You then receive a PIN, and confirm with a second SMS: RSC YES, the PIN, and a contact number.
How much does the SSC board challenge cost?
The fee is about 150 Taka per subject, deducted from your Teletalk balance. Subjects with two papers, such as Bangla and English, count as one subject for the fee. Keep enough balance before you start.
When can I apply for re-scrutiny after the SSC Result 2026?
The window opens the day after results and usually runs about a week. With the SSC Result 2026 confirmed for 10 August, expect the board challenge window to open around 11 August 2026. Confirm the exact dates on your board notice.
Does re-scrutiny always change my result?
No. Most applications do not change a grade, because the board only corrects genuine errors such as unmarked answers or addition mistakes. If your marks change, your GPA and marksheet are updated and your roll appears in the re-scrutiny result PDF.
